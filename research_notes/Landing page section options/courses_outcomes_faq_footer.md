## Q1. Best pricing/plan comparison layouts on editorial or minimal sites (not SaaS-looking), applied to COURSES (3-month vs 6-month)

### Takeaway
Fashion and design schools barely do side-by-side comparison. Polimoda and JD Institute use filtered card lists, and Pearl uses a "key facts" strip (duration / eligibility / location). That leaves room for a tailoring-native comparison object. The strongest fit is the **sewing-pattern envelope pair**, because a real pattern envelope already is a spec sheet. The **tape-measure timeline** works well as a secondary device that shows the 6-month course is literally longer. Avoid the SaaS look: no feature checkmark grid, no "Most popular" pill, no toggle.

### Cited Findings
- Polimoda's course listing uses image cards with a level label and title. Filters cover Level, Department, Subject and Career path. There is no compare tool or side-by-side view, and cards don't show durations — [Polimoda courses](https://www.polimoda.com/courses) (200)
- Pearl Academy's certificate page puts a labelled key-facts block under the hero: Duration "11 Months", Eligibility "Age 17 Years & Above", Location. Fees sit behind a fee calculator / EMI section rather than in the key-facts strip — [Pearl Academy Fashion Design for Womenswear](https://pearlacademy.com/programs/certificate-courses/fashion/fashion-design-womenswear) (200)
- Pearl's fashion certificate courses come in 6-month and 11-month lengths. That is a close analogue to a 3-month / 6-month pair, and Pearl presents them as separate course pages, not a comparison — [Pearl blog, short-term fashion courses](https://www.pearlacademy.com/blog/fashion/short-term-fashion-designing-courses)
- JD Institute's homepage lists courses as linked titles with no durations or fees on the page. CTAs are "Apply Now", "Download Brochure" and "Course Fees" (a separate page) — [JD Institute](https://www.jdinstitute.edu.in/) (200)
- Awwwards' "Pricing Page" element collection includes minimal, flat pricing-card examples such as Atwww Studio, Dora Alpha 2.0 (tagged price comparison) and Artboard Studio — [Awwwards: Pricing Page, Atwww Studio](https://www.awwwards.com/inspiration/pricing-page-atwww-studio) (200); [Awwwards: Dora Alpha 2.0](https://www.awwwards.com/inspiration/pricing-page-dora-alpha-2-0); [Awwwards: Artboard Studio](https://www.awwwards.com/inspiration/pricing-page-artboard-studio)
- Pattern envelopes carry technical drawings and variations on the back. Tilly and the Buttons' redesign aimed for "one striking image" on the front and a smaller alternative image on the back, to make shoppers "turn the pattern over". Early concepts used a garment on a hanger with a monochrome graphic border — [Tilly and the Buttons: Evolution of our pattern envelopes](https://www.tillyandthebuttons.com/blogs/sewing/sewing-pattern-envelopes) (200)
- Vintage envelope archives (browsable by decade/designer) and free public-domain envelope graphics exist for reference — [Vintage Patterns Wiki](https://vintagepatterns.fandom.com/wiki/McCall_693) (bot-blocked); [Old Design Shop vintage skirt pattern envelope](https://olddesignshop.com/2014/11/vintage-skirt-pattern-envelope-free-graphics/)
- A real-world precedent for care-label typography as a layout system (packaging, not web): a laundry product redesign whose layout mimics fabric care labels — [Packaging of the World (Derrick Lin)](https://packagingoftheworld.com/?p=26130)
- A restrained, functional-yet-warm type approach on a fashion e-commerce site (Ahluwalia, LL Bradford) — [Fonts In Use: Ahluwalia e-commerce](https://www.fontsinuse.com/uses/41898/ahluwalia-e-commerce)

### Inferences: COURSES section options
1. **Two pattern envelopes, side by side (recommended hero option).** Two clay-white paper envelopes, styled like 1950s Simplicity/Vogue/McCall's packaging, with black ink only. The front carries a big number ("03" / "06" months), "Pattern No. TT-03 / TT-06", a line drawing of the blouse (3-month) and the blouse plus lehenga (6-month), and a "Difficulty: Beginner" box. On hover or tap the envelope flips (CSS 3D rotateY) to the back. The back is a real pattern-back spec table: "Stages included" (the 5 stages as rows, with the 6-month adding extra rows), "You will need" (notions list: tape, chalk, kainchi, thread), hours per week, batch timings and fee. A "Choose this pattern" button goes to a prefilled WhatsApp message for that course. Why it works: the envelope's own format (front = promise, back = spec) is the comparison table, so it reads as editorial, not SaaS. Inspiration: Tilly envelope evolution, Vintage Patterns Wiki, Old Design Shop graphic.
2. **Tape-measure timeline.** One long horizontal tape (inch/cm ticks, a brass end-clip) runs across the section. The 3-month course occupies ticks 0-12 (weeks). The 6-month course keeps going to 24, and the extra length unrolls on scroll (scrubbed via GSAP ScrollTrigger or CSS scroll-driven animation). The 5 stages sit as chalk marks on the tape, and the 6-month section marks where the extra content goes (exact extras TBD from the client). On mobile the tape runs vertically. Why: duration is the main difference, and the tape shows it with zero copy. Best used as the band above the envelopes, or inside option 1's back panel. Inspiration: Framer/21st.dev scroll-stack components show the pinned-scroll mechanic — [Framer Scroll Stack](https://www.framer.com/marketplace/components/scroll-stack/); [21st.dev Stacking Cards](https://21st.dev/@danielpetho/components/stacking-cards.md).
3. **Two hanging price tags (swing tickets) on a thread.** Kraft/clay-white tags hang from a black thread with a safety pin, swaying slightly on scroll (a small CSS rotate keyed to scroll velocity). Each tag reads like a garment ticket: style no., "Size: 3 months", price ₹____, and a barcode that encodes the WhatsApp link (decorative). Why: price tags are the universal "what does it cost" signifier for a local audience. Fees are a top question for Indian course buyers (Pearl puts fees and EMI in their own block, see above). Risk: tags hold little text, so pair them with a care-label detail panel.
4. **Care labels as spec sheets.** Two long woven-label strips (satin texture, stitched ends), with the stage list printed down each label in a condensed sans, plus laundry-style icons redrawn as course icons (tape = measure, triangle = drafting, needle = stitch). The 6-month label is physically twice as long. Why: same "length = duration" logic as the tape, in a smaller footprint that works on mobile. Inspiration: Derrick Lin care-label packaging; Ahluwalia type.
- **Toggle switch (3M / 6M)**: not recommended as the primary layout. A toggle hides one option, and for only two options side by side compares better (see the NN/g principle in Q2 about not hiding content users need). If used at all, keep it mobile-only, styled as a tape-measure slider.

## Q2. Creative FAQ and footer designs (galleries) and FAQ usability, applied to FAQ and FINAL CTA + FOOTER

### Takeaway
NN/g says accordions suit FAQs (independent sections, rarely needed all at once). Headings must be accurate, the whole heading must be clickable, several panels should be openable at once, and critical info must stay outside the accordion. So fees, duration and timings must also appear in the Courses and Studio sections, not only in the FAQ. For footers, the current gallery trend is a giant edge-to-edge wordmark plus a small-type utility row. footer.design has a dedicated "Large Type" category.

### Cited Findings
- NN/g: accordions suit cases where "sections are independent and users rarely need several at once (e.g. FAQs)" and where content is long on a small screen. Avoid them when users need most of the content or when critical information would be buried. Write accurate headings, use a caret/plus signifier, make heading and icon both clickable, allow multiple panels open, offer expand/collapse all, use button semantics and announce state to screen readers — [NN/g: Accordions on Desktop](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/accordions-on-desktop/) (200)
- NN/g: on mobile, accordions give an overview and let users jump to what they want, but can cause disorientation and excess scrolling — [NN/g video: Accordions on Mobile](https://www.nngroup.com/videos/accordions-on-mobile/) (200)
- Pearl Academy's course FAQ is an accordion covering course, scope, eligibility, fees, application, careers, scholarships and software. The "Career Opportunities" answer sits inside the FAQ — [Pearl Academy course page](https://pearlacademy.com/programs/certificate-courses/fashion/fashion-design-womenswear) (200)
- One Page Love curates FAQ section patterns (accordion and grid): Kizupi, Pairio (FAQ grid), Dabbit, Midnight, Shelf, Flashform, Viewport — [One Page Love FAQ tag](https://onepagelove.com/tag/faq) (200); e.g. [Pairio FAQ grid](https://onepagelove.com/section/faq-grid/pairio), [Shelf FAQ accordion](https://onepagelove.com/section/faq-accordion/shelf)
- footer.design is a footer-only gallery with categories Typographic, Small Type, Illustrative, Grid, Animated, Cards, Dark, Large Type, Bold. Editor's Choice examples include Northeast Tomato, Trove, Aperios Design, Nothin', Decimals, Playfolly, Harvest Hall, The Design Society, Reality Is__ — [footer.design](https://www.footer.design/) (200); e.g. [northeasttomato.com](https://northeasttomato.com/), [thedesignsociety.fr](https://www.thedesignsociety.fr/), [noth.in](https://www.noth.in/)
- Abduzeedo's write-up of footer.design highlights Le Punch's footer: brand name in a massive condensed typeface, flipped vertically below a horizon line, with a live monospaced timestamp — [Abduzeedo: Footer Design Inspiration](https://abduzeedo.com/footer-design-inspiration-only-gallery-earth) (bot-blocked)
- Component-level references for a giant wordmark that bleeds off the bottom edge with a fade mask — [Framer footer section component](https://www.framer.com/community/marketplace/components/footer-section/); [21st.dev Kiln wordmark footer](https://21st.dev/@kedhareswer/components/kiln-wordmark-footer.md)
- Other galleries to browse for FAQ and footer patterns — [minimal.gallery](https://minimal.gallery/) (200), [httpster.net](https://httpster.net/) (200), [CSS Design Awards](https://www.cssdesignawards.com/) (200), [Awwwards fashion sites](https://www.awwwards.com/websites/fashion/) (200)

### Inferences: FAQ section options
1. **Tailor's order book / chit pad (recommended).** A ruled, carbon-copy order book (the kind Indian tailors use: "No. 0127", Name / Date / Measurements columns, perforated edge). Each FAQ is a numbered chit. Tapping a question tears off or slides out the "customer copy", and the answer appears below in handwriting-style or mono type. Questions should be real local ones: "I have never touched a sewing machine", "Do I need my own machine?", "Kannada/Hindi/English?", "Timings for working women / college students", "Fees and instalments", "Certificate?", "Can I start a boutique after?". Build it as a real `<button aria-expanded>` accordion (NN/g rules: whole row clickable, multiple open). Inspiration: Shelf/Dabbit accordion patterns on One Page Love.
2. **Measurement chart grid.** A tailor's measurement card (Bust / Waist / Shoulder / Sleeve rows) where each row is a question and the "measurement" cell holds the short answer, e.g. "Machine needed?" → "No — provided". Longer answers expand. Why: short answers stay visible without a click, which follows NN/g's "don't bury critical info". Inspiration: [Pairio FAQ grid](https://onepagelove.com/section/faq-grid/pairio).
3. **Pinned paper slips on a corkboard / pattern sheet.** Questions are scraps of pattern paper pinned with dressmaker pins. Click and the slip unfolds (a CSS clip-path reveal). This is the most playful option, but less accessible and harder to scan, so use it only for 5-6 FAQs.
4. **"Ask Shruti" chits leading to WhatsApp.** A standard accordion whose last item is a blank chit, "Your question isn't here? Write it on WhatsApp", opening wa.me with a prefilled "Hi, I have a question about the course: ". Combine it with option 1.

### Inferences: FINAL CTA + FOOTER options
1. **Giant wordmark as a tape measure.** "TEACHING THREADS" set edge to edge, bleeding off the bottom (the Framer/21st.dev pattern). A thin tape-measure ruler runs along the top edge of the wordmark with tick marks, and a hand-stitched running-stitch border (SVG dashed stroke, animated stroke-dashoffset on reveal) frames the footer. Above it is the final CTA: "Book a studio visit" and "WhatsApp Shruti". The small-type row holds the address, hours, Instagram, the phone number as a tel: link, and "Made in Hubballi". Inspiration: footer.design Large Type; Le Punch (Abduzeedo).
2. **Garment label footer.** The footer is one big woven brand label (the neck label sewn into every garment): logo center, "Est. ___ · Hubballi, Karnataka", a stitched rectangle border and fold shadow, with care-label icons as social links. The final CTA above it is a single oversized kainchi that "cuts" a dashed line when the CTA button is hovered or pressed. Ties to the existing kainchi prototype on this branch.
3. **Final CTA as an appointment chit.** A torn-off order-book slip: "Studio visit — Name ___ Preferred day ___ Batch ___". Filling the two or three fields composes a prefilled WhatsApp message (no backend form). This makes the WhatsApp message structured for the owner and continues the FAQ order-book motif.
4. **Sticky WhatsApp + Call bar (mobile) / floating thread spool (desktop).** On mobile, a fixed bottom bar with two buttons, "WhatsApp" and "Call". On desktop, a small floating spool icon bottom-right that unspools a label "Chat with us". Hide it while the final CTA section is in view so there are no duplicate buttons. See Q4 for evidence.

## Q3. How fashion schools present course options and careers, applied to AFTER THE COURSE (career outcomes)

### Takeaway
Big schools present careers as (a) a filter taxonomy (Polimoda's "Career path" filter), (b) a bulleted list inside the FAQ (Pearl), or (c) a career-service/stats block (Marangoni's "91% find work within one year", JD's "80% placement support"). Teaching Threads has no student work, testimonials or stats, so the section should show **concrete local paths** (boutique, home tailoring, alterations, bridal blouse specialist, further study) as objects, not numbers. Do not invent placement stats.

### Cited Findings
- Polimoda exposes a "Career path" filter on its course list (e.g. Fashion Buyer, Brand Manager, Trend Forecaster), so careers work as a browsing lens rather than a section — [Polimoda courses](https://www.polimoda.com/courses) (200)
- Pearl's womenswear certificate lists careers in an FAQ answer, including "Entrepreneurs", "Embroidery/Surface experts", "Heads of sampling departments", "Costume designers for stage or TV/movie projects" and "Personalised wedding trousseau planners" — [Pearl Academy course page](https://pearlacademy.com/programs/certificate-courses/fashion/fashion-design-womenswear) (200). Third-party listings for the same course also name "boutique owner" and "freelance designer" — [Careers360](https://www.careers360.com/colleges/pearl-academy-jaipur/fashion-design-for-womens-wear-certification-course)
- Pearl's certificate page cites "99% Placement Record in 2023" with T&C, which is dated and self-reported — [Pearl certificate fashion courses](https://pearlacademy.com/programs/certificate-courses/fashion)
- Istituto Marangoni presents a Career Service (coaching, mock interviews, job access via Symplicity) and cites a 2024 Doxa survey: 91% of students find work within one year — [Istituto Marangoni Career & Industry Relations](https://www.istitutomarangoni.com/career-and-industry-relations/students) (200)
- JD Institute leads with institutional numbers (45,000+ graduates, 80% placement support), and its own page is inconsistent (20+ branches vs 38+ centres) — [JD Institute](https://www.jdinstitute.edu.in/) (200)
- NIFT's main site, for reference on Indian institutional presentation — [NIFT](https://www.nift.ac.in/) (200)

### Inferences: AFTER THE COURSE options
1. **Clothes rail of career paths (recommended).** A horizontal rail (black steel line) with 5-6 clay-white hangers. Each hanger holds a garment-shaped card with a swing tag naming a path: "Open your own boutique", "Stitch from home for your neighbourhood", "Blouse & bridal specialist", "Alterations service", "Work at a design house / export unit", "Go on to a design degree". Drag or scroll pushes the hangers along the rail with a little swing physics. On mobile it becomes a horizontal snap scroller. Each tag's back gives "what you'd need next" (e.g. a machine plus a GST number for a boutique). Why: it mirrors Polimoda's career-path taxonomy and Pearl's list but as a physical object, with honest content and no stats needed.
2. **Pattern pieces assembling into a garment.** The pieces of a blouse pattern (front, back, sleeve x2) float apart, each labelled with a skill learned (Measure, Draft, Stitch...). On scroll they slide together into a finished blouse silhouette, and the assembled garment reveals "and now you can..." with 3-4 career paths. Why: it shows "skills combine into a livelihood" and reuses the 5-stage vocabulary. It is more complex to build; see the scroll-stack pins in Q1.
3. **Shop-front signboard mock.** A painted Indian shop signboard, "______ BOUTIQUE · Ladies Tailoring · Blouse Specialist", where the blank animates through names in a typewriter effect ("Your name here"). Below are 3 short path cards. Why: it is aspirational and very local (a familiar Hubballi street visual) and speaks to the dominant real outcome, self-employment.
4. **Order book of first customers.** An order-book ledger showing "your first orders": "Blouse — ₹___", "Kurti alteration — ₹___", "Lehenga — ₹___". Illustrative earning potential, clearly labelled as typical local charges. It only works if the client supplies real local rates, so it is a risky option. Never fabricate.

## Q4. Conversion best practices for WhatsApp-first Indian small businesses, applied to VISIT THE STUDIO and CTAs

### Takeaway
WhatsApp is the dominant business channel in India: per Meta, more than 60% of Indian WhatsApp users message a business weekly (Oct 2023). Use `wa.me/91XXXXXXXXXX?text=` links with a URL-encoded prefilled message per CTA (course-specific), plus a tel: link. For location, use a static map image or illustrated map that links to a Google Maps URL (no API key needed), and load the live iframe only on tap (facade). Hard conversion-lift numbers for sticky WhatsApp bars in India come only from vendor blogs, so treat them as unverified.

### Cited Findings
- Click-to-chat format: `https://wa.me/<number>?text=<urlencoded>`. The number must be full international format with no zeros, brackets or dashes (India: `91` + 10 digits). Spaces encode as `%20` and newlines as `%0A`; use `encodeURIComponent()`. The user still presses send. Links give no click analytics on their own — [Unipile: WhatsApp link API](https://www.unipile.com/es/?p=275019); [Bird: what is a click-to-chat link](https://bird.com/explained/whatsapp/what-is-a-whatsapp-click-to-chat-link). (The official WhatsApp FAQ page did not render for fetch: [faq.whatsapp.com](https://faq.whatsapp.com/5913398998672934).)
- Meta (Zuckerberg, Q3 2023 earnings): over 60% of WhatsApp users in India message a business account every week, and click-to-message ad revenue in India doubled year over year — [Sentinel Assam](https://sentinelassam.com/business/revenue-from-click-to-message-ads-in-india-has-doubled-meta-founder-672693); [exchange4media](https://www.exchange4media.com/digital-news/meta-indias-revenue-from-click-to-message-ads-double-in-fy23-130652.html) (bot-blocked)
- In Sept 2024 Meta launched Meta Verified and customized messages for small businesses in India, plus training on setting up click-to-WhatsApp ads — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/09/12/whatsapp-brings-meta-verified-customized-messages-to-small-businesses-in-india)
- Pearl Academy (a large Indian institute) offers "Connect on WhatsApp", "Call Us" (tel: link to an 1800 helpline) and an OTP-verified enquiry form side by side. So even big Indian institutes treat WhatsApp as a first-class CTA — [Pearl Academy course page](https://pearlacademy.com/programs/certificate-courses/fashion/fashion-design-womenswear) (200)
- Vendor claims (unverified, no method stated): a floating WhatsApp button lifts enquiries 20-40%, WhatsApp funnels convert 3-5x better than forms, and over 70% of traffic is mobile — [Prateeksha: CRO Mumbai](https://prateeksha.com/blog/conversion-rate-optimization-mumbai); [Prateeksha: WhatsApp funnel](https://prateeksha.com/whatsapp-funnel-setup). The general sticky CTA example (+12% add-to-cart, mostly mobile) is illustrative, not a controlled study — [Heatmap glossary: sticky CTA](https://heatmap.com/glossary/sticky-cta)
- Google Maps URLs need no API key. Search: `https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=...&query_place_id=...`; directions: `https://www.google.com/maps/dir/?api=1&destination=...` (travelmode includes `two-wheeler`) — [Google Maps URLs](https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/urls/get-started)
- The Maps Embed API is free with unlimited usage. The sample iframe uses `loading="lazy"` and a key parameter — [Maps Embed API](https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/embed/get-started) (200)
- `loading="lazy"` alone is not enough: Chrome starts fetching lazy iframes about 1,250px (fast connection) to 2,500px (slow) before the viewport. A facade (placeholder image swapped for the live map on interaction) can reach a 100% Lighthouse score — [CoreWebVitals.io: Google Maps 100% pagespeed](https://www.corewebvitals.io/pagespeed/google-maps-100-percent-pagespeed) (200)
- Lighthouse's "Lazy load third-party resources with facades" audit covers chat widgets and suggests lazy iframe loaders (e.g. vb/lazyframe) — [Chrome Lighthouse: third-party facades](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/lighthouse/performance/third-party-facades) (200); [lazyframe](https://github.com/vb/lazyframe)

### Inferences: VISIT THE STUDIO options
1. **Map drawn as a pattern sheet (recommended).** A hand-drawn SVG map of the studio's neighbourhood in Hubballi, styled like a printed pattern sheet: grey grid, dashed cutting lines for roads, notches and grainline arrows for direction, a landmark labelled "Piece A: ___ Circle". The studio is marked with a chalk X / dressmaker pin. Tapping it opens the Google Maps directions URL (`dir/?api=1&destination=...&travelmode=two-wheeler`). There is no iframe, so no performance cost, and it is fully on-brand. An optional "Open live map" button loads the iframe facade.
2. **Hours as a care label.** A long satin care label with the hours in care-instruction grammar: "MON–SAT 10:00–1:00 / 4:00–7:00 · SUNDAY CLOSED · Batches: morning / evening · Visit without appointment: yes/no". Small care icons are redrawn (iron = morning batch, moon = evening). Beside it sits a photo of the actual street/storefront (owner photos exist in the repo), which builds trust for a first visit.
3. **Studio visit as a "fitting appointment" card.** A tailor's appointment card: "Your first fitting at Teaching Threads", with day chips (Mon–Sat) and time chips. Selecting them composes a wa.me message: "Hi Shruti, I'd like to visit the studio on Thursday evening. Course: 3-month." The address, a "Get directions" link and a tel: link sit underneath. Highest conversion intent and no backend.
4. **POV walk-in.** A short Kling/scroll clip, POV walking from the street through the studio door to the cutting table (continuing the POV clay-object video language), ending on the address and hours label. Why: it removes the "where is it / what's inside" fear for local beginners. Keep the poster frame static for performance.

### Inferences: CTA mechanics (all sections)
- Use one wa.me link per intent with distinct prefilled text, e.g. "Hi, I'm interested in the 3-month course", "...6-month course", "I'd like to book a studio visit", "I have a question". The owner sees the source immediately, which also stands in for missing click analytics.
- Mobile: a sticky bottom bar [WhatsApp | Call], shown after the hero and hidden over the final CTA. Desktop: a small floating button. Plain `<a>` links need no third-party widget script, so no facade is needed.
- Keep fees, duration and timings visible outside the FAQ (NN/g, Q2).
